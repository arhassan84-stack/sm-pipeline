ROUND 4: BALANCED TRAINING (d<1 downsampled to match d>=1 count)
================================================================================
Motivation: d<1 (63.5% of data) vs d>=1 (36.5%) imbalance at d=1 threshold.
Finding: log(d) is PERFECTLY UNIFORM by design (5,000 samples per log-bin).
         The 63.5%/36.5% split is geometric, not a data generation artifact.
         The d>=1 MAPE gap (20.5% vs 12.0%) reflects intrinsic physics difficulty,
         not data scarcity: fast diffusion has shorter ACF tails → harder features.
Strategy: downsample d<1 to 28,595 = d>=1 count → 57,190 balanced training samples
          (27% reduction from 90k).

--- Round 7: Ensemble weight optimization ---

Approach: scipy.differential_evolution + Nelder-Mead minimizing MAPE on TRAINING data
          (softmax parametrization for unconstrained optimization); grid search on test
          as oracle upper bound. Two objectives: overall MAPE and d>=1-focused (2x weight).

Key finding: optimizer collapses weights onto CNN (~80%), hurting R² (0.9179→0.9114).
Root cause: correlated predictions make the training-loss surface ill-conditioned; the
optimizer overfits to training distribution rather than finding a generalizable solution.

Grid search (test oracle, step=0.02):

  - Optimal weights give FTT more weight (~32-40%) than equal 1/3, slightly less to CNN.
  - Best grid result: resnet+cnn+ftt [0.10, 0.58, 0.32] → MAPE=13.65%, d>=1=19.2%.

  - scipy optimizer cannot be used reliably — overfits to train; grid search on test
    confirms the equal-weight ensemble is already well-calibrated.

           True TTA would require GPU-side noise on raw traces — not worth the compute cost.
